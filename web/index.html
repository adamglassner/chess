How to Play

Each side starts with sixteen pieces. White moves first, then the players take turns. The goal is checkmate: attack the enemy king so that it has no way to escape.

  1. Moving. Click a piece and then a square, or drag it there. Dots show where it can go; rings mark captures.
  2. The pieces. The king moves one square any way. The queen slides any distance in any direction. The rook slides along ranks and files, the bishop along diagonals. The knight jumps in an L shape and can leap over pieces. A pawn steps forward one square (two on its first move) and captures one square diagonally forward.
  3. Check. When a king is attacked it is in check, and its side must get out of check straight away.
  4. Castling. Once per game, if the king and a rook haven't moved, the squares between are empty and the king isn't in, through or into check, the king moves two squares toward the rook and the rook hops over it. Drag the king two squares to castle.
  5. En passant. If a pawn moves two squares and lands beside an enemy pawn, that pawn may capture it as if it had moved one square, but only on the very next move.
  6. Promotion. A pawn that reaches the far side becomes a queen, rook, bishop or knight.
  7. Draws. Stalemate (no legal move but not in check), the same position three times, fifty moves each without a capture or pawn move, too little material to mate, or a draw offer that's accepted.

Six members of the Ravenscroft Library will give you a game, from Pip the Page (just learning) to the Old Master. Your record against each of them is kept, and an unfinished game is waiting for you when you come back.
